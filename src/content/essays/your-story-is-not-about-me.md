Taking things personally is when we start believing that everything people say or do has something to do with us.

We let their behavior decide how we see ourselves and how much we think we’re worth. And sometimes, when we don’t know how to love ourselves enough, we look to other people to fill that gap. We expect them to give us the validation, care, or reassurance we’re missing.

But the truth is, people won’t always give us exactly what we want or need.

What people do is usually a reflection of who they are, not a definition of who we are. And the same goes for us. The way we treat someone comes from who we are, not always from what that person has done.

Someone being distant doesn’t mean you’re not worth being close to.\
Someone leaving doesn’t mean you weren’t enough.\
Someone treating you badly doesn’t mean you deserved it.

Everyone is living through their own story, carrying their own fears, experiences, and struggles.

Taking things personally is almost like saying:

“Your story is about me.”

But it’s not.

Sometimes, the healthiest thing we can do is let people be who they are without making their behavior a reflection of our worth.

And maybe that’s where a little peace begins.
